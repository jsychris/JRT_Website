# Jersey Round Table — Research Dossier

Background research compiled for the Jersey Round Table website project.
Date of compilation: 2026-05-14.

This document collates publicly available references to **Jersey Round Table** (JRT) from news, charity directories, archive catalogues and social channels. Each section is sourced — see footnotes/links inline. Where a primary source could not be confirmed, this is flagged explicitly so it can be verified directly with the JRT committee or by visiting Jersey Archive in person.

---

## 1. Quick summary

- **Jersey Round Table** is the Jersey chapter of **Round Table Great Britain & Ireland** (RTBI), itself a member of **Round Table International**.
- Founded in **1948**, making the chapter **77 years old in 2025** (78 in 2026).
- A men's service club for **ages 18–45**, non-political and non-religious.
- Combines **community service** (volunteering on local sites, environmental work) with **fundraising** (annual Charity Ball, Santa float, casino nights, bag packs) and **social fellowship** (meetings every other Tuesday, international travel).
- Current Chair (as of late 2024 / 2025): **Christian Spurr** — described as a member of "over a decade".
- Sister organisations on island that form the wider **Round Table family**: Ladies' Circle (18–45 women), 41 Club (ex-Tablers over 40), Tangent (ex-Circlers).

## 2. Founding and history

### 2.1 Date of founding
- **1948** — established as a chapter of the broader Round Table movement.
- This places Jersey Round Table just **one year before Guernsey Round Table**, which was chartered in 1949 with C.A. Travers as its first president.
- Both chapters sit within the post-war wave of UK Round Table expansion that followed the parent body's founding in Norwich on **14 March 1927** by **Louis Marchesi** (full name Erminio William Louis Marchesi, 1898–1968).

### 2.2 Founders
- Founded by "a small group of close friends" — names beyond Brian Le Marquand are not yet documented in publicly indexed sources and should be confirmed via Jersey Archive (see §6) or club records.
- **Brian Le Marquand** is identified as the founding figure. He later became **national president of Round Table Britain and Ireland (RTBI)** — a notable honour for a chapter from a small Crown Dependency.
- Brian Le Marquand also ran a horticultural supplies shop on the corner of Beresford Street and Cattle Street in St Helier until his retirement in 1976 — a useful biographical detail for any "history of the club" page.

### 2.3 Parent body context (worth referencing on the site)
- Round Table was founded by Louis Marchesi, then a young Norwich Rotary Club member, who wanted a forum for younger businessmen.
- The motto comes from a 1927 speech by the Prince of Wales: *"Adopt methods that have proved so sound in the past, Adapt them to the changing needs of the time, and wherever possible, Improve them."* — the source of "Adopt, Adapt, Improve".
- The Round Table emblem alludes to the historic table at Winchester's Great Hall, **not** Arthurian legend.
- Growth: 85 members within a year; 4,600 across 125 tables by 1939; first international table in Copenhagen in 1936; global by the late 20th century.

### 2.4 Members of note (named in public sources)
The Bailiwick Express FOCUS piece (Oct 2024) says JRT has counted among its members:
- The **current Bailiff** of Jersey.
- A **past Chief Minister** of Jersey.
*(The article does not name them; needs cross-referencing with JRT's own records.)*

## 3. What Jersey Round Table actually does

### 3.1 Membership
- **Men aged 18–45**. Members come from a wide spread of professional backgrounds — IT, graphic design, farming, entrepreneurship, law — explicitly framed as cross-sector.
- **Meet every other Tuesday.**
- Member philosophy as quoted by the Chair: *"It's about forming lifelong friendships, developing new skills, and finding purpose in giving back."*

### 3.2 Flagship events and traditions

**Santa Float ("Santa visits")**
- Decades-old tradition: Santa Claus is escorted around all 12 parishes on a red sleigh during the run-up to Christmas.
- Doubles as a major collection: proceeds go to the **Jersey Charities Appeal**.
- Described in the FOCUS piece as the **most well-known** of JRT's traditions and a touchstone of childhood memory for many islanders.

**Annual Charity Ball**
- The chapter's signature fundraiser.
- Recent venue: **Royal Jersey Showground**, Trinity.
- Typical scale: **400–500 guests**, black-tie, minimum table size 8.
- Format: pre-dinner entertainment, three-course dinner, themed performance by members ("a special show"), live band, raffle.
- **Headline sponsor: warm.je** (multi-year).
- **Catering: Harpers Catering / Simon Harper** (named on the 2024 ball).
- **Band: Inside Job** (recurring on recent line-ups).

| Year | Date | Notes / beneficiaries |
|---|---|---|
| 2018 (sample) | 24 Nov | Brightly, Down's Syndrome Jersey, Autism Jersey, Jersey Women's Refuge — £85/head |
| 2020 | — | **Cancelled** (COVID-19) |
| 2021 | 27 Nov | **Cancelled** (Jersey reported 910 active COVID cases) — beneficiaries had been Mind Jersey, Jersey Heart Support Group, Causeway |

**Environmental / community service**
- **Governor's Walk at La Gigoulande Mill, St Peter's Valley.** Major multi-year project to make the historic Crown mill site accessible to the public.
  - JRT approached former HM Receiver General **David Pett** in **2019** to volunteer on the conservation project.
  - Worked on the **millpond refurbishment**: felled sycamores and scrub willow, exposed the deteriorated dam wall, engaged specialist stone masons, cleared the pond bed (removed "several loads of rubbish"), installed a sluice gate from a local auction, created a spillway in spring 2021.
  - Opened by then-Lieutenant-Governor **Air Chief Marshal Sir Stephen Dalton** in December 2020.
  - Steps and walkways installed by JRT make the scenic stretch (from St Peter's Valley up towards St Mary) accessible to the public.
  - The connecting **400-metre footpath** linking the mill to the National Trust for Jersey HQ at The Elms was opened by Lieutenant-Governor **Vice-Admiral Jerry Kyd** in December 2022; that path was built by the Probation Service's community service crews and fenced with funding from the **Roy Overland Charitable Trust**, with JRT having delivered the millpond element.
  - Historical note for site copy: Gigoulande was a Crown mill first registered in 1247 and one of only four watermills in the British Isles known to have used **two overshot wheels** (upper feeding lower).
- **Annual environmental day**: clearing invasive species in support of the **National Trust for Jersey**.

**Other fundraising activities**
- Casino nights.
- Supermarket bag packing.

### 3.3 Fellowship and the wider Round Table network
- Members attend **RTBI National Sporting Weekend** events.
- Regular fellowship trips to **Switzerland, France and Sark** for joint events with other Tables.

## 9. Useful angles for the website build

Patterns and content hooks that fall out of this research:

1. **Lead with the 1948 date and Brian Le Marquand.** A 77-year heritage and a Jerseyman who became RTBI national president is a compelling, locally-rooted founding story — much stronger than a generic "we're a service club" intro.
2. **Photo-led "history" page** sourced from the **JEP Photographic Archive** via Jersey Heritage. The 57 indexed records are essentially a free archive of Charity Balls, Santa floats and presidents going back decades.
3. **The Santa float is the most recognisable thing about JRT** — make it a hero artefact on the site (calendar of parish dates, donations route, photos of the float).
4. **Gigoulande Mill is the strongest "we built something that lasted" story** — a multi-year environmental win with named Lieutenant-Governors at both ribbon-cuttings. Worth a dedicated case-study page.
5. **Charity Ball deserves its own subdomain-style landing page** — sponsors, beneficiaries, ticket sign-up, archive of past years' beneficiaries and totals.
6. **Localised stats** — total raised since 1948, number of charities funded, parishes visited, environmental hours — these are the credibility levers the parent RTBI site fails to pull. JRT should pull them.
7. **Member stories** — every Bailiwick FOCUS piece quoted the Chair only; a website is the place to put 4–6 short member testimonials with faces and trades.
8. **Three-track audience nav** — "Thinking of joining?", "Apply for funding / nominate a cause", "Members area" — same pattern Rotary uses, lifted in `CLAUDE.md`.
